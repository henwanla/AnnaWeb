THE DETAILS / PLANNING NOTES

The details behind the idea.

Practical planning guidance for the details that often get lost between a first idea and a clear quotation.

✳

The detail makes
the difference.

Read one guide or use them together. The right next step depends on what you already know.

Put it into a brief →
01What belongs in a project brief?+

Record the product or the choice still needed, total quantity and quantity per design, artwork status, packing preference, receiving point and usable-by date. Mark unknowns so the next reply can resolve them.

Keep the model and brief version together. A general event description alone may leave printing, quantities and delivery open to different interpretations.

02How should artwork be checked?+

Confirm the actual model template, printing sides and restricted areas first. Check bitmap pixels against the final placed size; a fan’s closed length is not automatically the artwork width.

A PPI calculation does not approve print quality, colour or text readability. Record the exact artwork version and what a digital or physical approval covers.

Use the resolution calculator →
03How do I compare quotations fairly?+

Start with the same product, printing, quantities, packing and receiving point. Ask which setup, sample, design, shipping and import costs are included, separate, excluded or still unknown.

Keep the currency and quotation version visible. Unknown costs should stay unknown rather than silently becoming zero.

Use the quotation comparison tool →
04How do I plan around an event date?+

Choose when the finished items must be received, checked and ready to distribute. Confirm the start conditions for artwork, any sample approval, production and shipping.

Check calendar versus working days, relevant cutoffs and what the estimate excludes. Keep receiving and event preparation in the plan, with unresolved stages clearly marked.

A FEW THINGS WORTH CONFIRMING

Before the
final decision.

Does one minimum apply to every fan?+

Confirm the actual model, printing or other changes, and quantity per design. An existing design, a handle logo and a newly printed fan face may have different requirements.

Is a digital mockup the same as a sample?+

A digital mockup can show a layout. A physical sample can support different checks. Confirm what each approval covers, which version it applies to and what changes need another review.

Does “ready to ship” mean ready for my event?+

Shipping is a separate stage. Confirm the receiving point, transport estimate and any excluded stages, then allow for receiving checks and distribution before the usable-by date.
